PRISM: Plug-&-Forget Smart Health Toilet Sensor

PREP/T-PREP Design Expo · Design team; housing, assembly, and manufacturing · Jul – Aug 2025
Tools: OnShape, 3D printing, laser cutting · Best Prototype and People's Choice

Chronic kidney disease affects around 850 million people worldwide, and catching it early can depend on noticing changes in hydration and urine chemistry before symptoms show up. A toilet-mounted sensor makes it possible to collect this data passively without requiring users to change their routine or test themselves manually.

What I built

Our team built PRISM, a clip-on device that mounts to a toilet seat and passively tracks hydration and pH using color and pH sensors. I designed the housing in OnShape, assembled the prototype around both sensors, and manufactured the parts using 3D printing and laser cutting. While existing hydration trackers can cost $300–500+ and may require disposable cartridges, our prototype cost under $50 and also included app integration and more urine chemistry data.

Housing and CAD design

The clip is designed to hook over the toilet rim underneath the seat. The current prototype is not mounted that way yet, but that is the intended final setup. All of the electronics are enclosed inside the housing, with acrylic covers making the unit splashproof. Size was not a main concern for this prototype, so it is larger than a final version would need to be. The housing is angled so the sensors point in the right direction for an average toilet, while the pH sensor can be adjusted vertically to account for different water levels. The entire unit can also be lifted straight out and reattached without any tools.

Assembly

Assembly took a few iterations because the housing and 3D-printed parts were designed before we had the actual sensors and electronics. The first prints were too large, so I resized the parts once we had the real dimensions. 3D printing was chosen for this stage because it allowed for rapid, low-cost prototyping while we worked through those iterations. After that, the electronics were bolted into place inside the housing, and the acrylic covers were glued on to keep the unit splashproof.

Laser cutting

I laser cut two acrylic covers, one for the electronics housing and one for the color sensor housing, and sealed both to keep them splashproof. I also designed the PRISM logo and engraved it directly onto the electronics cover.

Design expo and awards

I presented a prototype to 100+ industry and academic professionals at the PREP/TPREP Design Expo. We were awarded People's Choice and Best Prototype.
