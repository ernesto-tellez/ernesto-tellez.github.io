Chronic kidney disease affects an estimated 850 million people worldwide, and early identification often comes down to catching subtle changes in hydration and urine chemistry before symptoms appear. A toilet-mounted sensor is a practical way to capture that data passively, without asking anyone to change their routine or actively test themselves.

OnShape 3D Printing Laser Cutting Sensors
Overview

What I Built

Our team built PRISM, a clip-on sensor that mounts to a toilet seat and passively monitors hydration and pH through color and pH sensors. My role was designing the housing in OnShape, assembling the prototype around the two sensors, and manufacturing the parts through 3D printing and laser cutting. Compared to existing hydration trackers — which run $300–500+ per unit, some with recurring costs for disposable cartridges — our prototype cost under $50 while adding app integration and fuller urine chemistry data than competitors offered.
